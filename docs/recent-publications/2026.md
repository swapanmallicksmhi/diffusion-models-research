## Diffusion-Based Data Assimilation

### Using Diffusion Models to Do Data Assimilation

**Authors:** Daniel Hodyss and Matthias Morzfeld

**Journal:** Monthly Weather Review

**Year:** 2026

**Volume:** 154, Issue 2

**DOI:** [10.1175/MWR-D-25-0125.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-25-0125.1)

**Publication status:** Peer-reviewed research article

**Keywords:** Diffusion models, data assimilation,
Bayesian inference, posterior sampling.

#### Scientific Overview

This study examines the theoretical formulation of
diffusion-based data assimilation methods, with particular
attention to the posterior probability distributions
targeted by different assimilation approaches.

The analysis distinguishes between diffusion-based
assimilation formulations according to their prior
distributions, likelihood functions, training requirements,
and resulting state estimates.

An important consideration is the relationship between
the learned probabilistic representation and the
assimilation target distribution.

#### Relevance to Atmospheric Data Assimilation

The study provides a useful theoretical perspective for
evaluating the integration of generative diffusion models
into atmospheric data assimilation systems.

It is particularly relevant to Bayesian posterior
sampling, flow-dependent uncertainty, and the
computational requirements of diffusion-based analysis.

## AI Weather and Earth System Modeling

### Scaling Storm-Resolving Atmospheric AI Simulation to the Entire Planet

**Authors:** Zeyuan Hu, Akshay Subramaniam, Noel Keen, Tao Ge, Jaideep Pathak, Mohammad Shoaib Abbas, Suman Ravuri, Karthik Kashinath, Naser Mahfouz, Peter Caldwell, Mike Pritchard, and Noah Brenowitz

**Year:** 2026

**Publication status:** arXiv preprint

**Research category:** Global Storm-Resolving AI Modeling

**arXiv:** [2606.31248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31248)

**PDF:** [Download paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31248)

#### Scientific Overview

This study introduces STRATA, a global storm-resolving
atmospheric AI emulator designed to represent
kilometer-scale atmospheric dynamics.

The model employs an autoregressive transformer
architecture trained on high-resolution atmospheric
simulations produced by the SCREAM model.

The training dataset consists of approximately 17 days
of global atmospheric simulation at 4.9-km resolution,
with outputs sampled every 10 minutes.

#### Main Scientific Contributions

- Development of a global storm-resolving AI emulator.
- Representation of kilometer-scale atmospheric dynamics.
- Tile-based autoregressive modeling.
- Use of local three-dimensional attention mechanisms.
- Improved computational efficiency compared with
  computationally expensive storm-resolving simulations.

#### Relevance to Earth System Sciences

This work demonstrates the potential of AI models
for computationally efficient, high-resolution
global atmospheric simulation.

It provides a useful reference for evaluating
AI-based representations of convective-scale processes
and the computational requirements of Earth system
foundation models.

### Missing the Butterfly and Predicting the Past: Features or Bugs of Accurate AI Weather Models?

**Authors:** Pedram Hassanzadeh, Weidong Li, Y. Qiang Sun, Jiangdi Wang, Alexander Wikner, Justin Finkel, and Jonathan Q. Weare

**Year:** 2026

**Publication status:** arXiv preprint

**Research category:** AI Weather Prediction and Predictability

**arXiv:** [2608.25835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25835)

**PDF:** [Download paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25835)

#### Scientific Overview

This study investigates the relationship between
the predictive accuracy of AI weather models and
their representation of atmospheric predictability,
chaotic dynamics, and physical consistency.

The analysis examines forecasting and backcasting
capabilities across a hierarchy of dynamical
systems, including atmospheric reanalysis,
general circulation models, and the Lorenz system.

Particular attention is given to the influence
of coarse-graining in training datasets on
the learned dynamics of AI prediction models.

#### Main Scientific Contributions

- Investigation of the physical fidelity of AI
  weather prediction models.
- Analysis of forward prediction and backcasting.
- Examination of the butterfly effect in AI models.
- Assessment of coarse-graining and unresolved scales.
- Discussion of implications for atmospheric
  predictability and long-term climate emulation.

#### Relevance to Earth System Sciences

The study highlights important questions concerning
the interpretation of AI weather model accuracy
and the representation of multiscale chaotic dynamics.

These findings are relevant to the evaluation
of generative and deterministic AI models,
particularly when assessing physical consistency,
forecast uncertainty, and predictability.
